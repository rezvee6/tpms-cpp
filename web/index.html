How it works

1 · Tooth edges in

Each ABS tone ring has 48 teeth. An ISR timestamps every edge from a 10 MHz timer into a lock-free queue (≈3–7k edges/s). The detector learns each tooth's machining error and resamples rim speed to a uniform 250 Hz.

2 · Rolling radius

A soft tire has a smaller rolling radius and spins faster. Diagonal, side and axle speed ratios are compared with a baseline (regressed on speed² and acceleration) learned after a TPMS reset. Cornering is removed using the yaw sensor.

3 · Resonance

The sidewall is a torsional spring. A 256-point FFT of rim speed shows a peak near 40 Hz that drops as pressure falls. This gives an absolute estimate, so it can detect all four tires losing pressure together, which the ratio method can't.

Constraints

Written for a Cortex-M4F: 32 KB RAM budget enforced by static_assert, no heap, no exceptions or RTTI, float only, twiddle and window tables computed at compile time, and wrap-safe 32-bit timer arithmetic.
